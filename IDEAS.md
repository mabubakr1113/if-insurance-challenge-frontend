# Idea evaluation — If challenge "If Insurance Could Do More"

JunctionX Turku · compiled 2026-10-16

All ideas we discussed, the evidence behind them, what already exists, and how each scores against the four judging criteria. Updated after the review and the decision to proceed with If Home Watch.

## Selected direction — If Home Watch

**Decision:** build and pitch idea #8. The first version focuses on a family looking after a parent's home after a death, using the clear life-event story from #3. Cottages, moves into care and other long absences are later expansion opportunities.

**Customer promise:** "Know what needs attention, who is handling it, and how to get help when nobody can visit."

**First-version scope:** confirm that the home is empty and who can arrange access; create an ordered care plan; assign visits and urgent tasks; keep photos and house instructions together; turn weather warnings into actions; offer an approved local visit when the family needs help; record what was checked.

**Business direction:** test optional paid visits and a transparent coordination fee alongside the expected benefits of prevention and customer continuity. Prices, partner availability, demand and margins still need validation.

The family receives practical help and a record of completed checks. Claim-related evidence is a supporting benefit. Any proposed acceptance of particular records by If requires an agreed evidence standard; the app does not promise full reimbursement.

Selection reflects the team's chosen direction. The provisional scores below are retained honestly rather than increased to make the selected idea rank first.

## Judging criteria (from the challenge brief)

1. **Customer value and relevance:** a real, clearly defined human need tied to a meaningful life event; value easy to understand.
2. **Business value for If:** a credible mechanism (retention, acquisition, risk prevention, fewer claims, efficiency, new value).
3. **Feasibility and scalability:** show how the technology, data, partners and capabilities would work in practice.
4. **Innovation and experience:** challenges the traditional role of insurance; more than a generic insurance app or an incremental improvement.

Key lines from the brief: insurance should not be the centre of the experience; "AI, data and automation can be part of the solution, but technology is not the goal"; "the most sustainable claim is the one that never needs to happen" (If's own description).

## Scoring rubric

The brief publishes no weights. Equal weighting is a working comparison assumption, not an official judging rule (total out of 20). Half points are allowed, but these are provisional judgements; small differences should not decide the project.

| Score | 1. Customer value | 2. Business value | 3. Feasibility | 4. Innovation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | A major life-event need demonstrated by customer evidence, with clear value | A credible mechanism supported by validated incremental economics | Delivery and scaling demonstrated, including operations and partners | A clearly differentiated experience with demonstrated customer benefit |
| 4 | Clear life event and need; customer validation still limited | Clear, credible levers; size and economics rest on assumptions | Components exist; some new operations or integrations are needed | A meaningful change in the insurer's role, with differentiation still to validate |
| 3 | Plausible need, with relevance or perceived value still uncertain | A testable mechanism with substantial commercial uncertainty | Significant new capabilities or adoption assumptions remain | A useful combination or extension of existing services |
| 2 | Weak evidence that customers value the proposed solution | An indirect or poorly supported mechanism | Major unresolved delivery blockers | Limited improvement over existing services |
| 1 | No clear customer need | No credible mechanism | No credible delivery path | No meaningful differentiation |

Prevention, retention, acquisition and service revenue are all valid business benefits under the brief. The number of revenue levers, size relative to all If premiums and absence of a competitor in a search do not establish a higher score by themselves.

### Review findings applied to the decision

- **Home Watch:** confirm vacancy and authority before arranging access. A death notice is an invitation opportunity, not evidence that a home is empty. Family coordination and partner delivery still require adoption.
- **Claim evidence:** replace the blanket payout promise with records of specific checks. Coverage, deductibles, age deductions and other policy conditions continue to matter.
- **Maintenance services:** If Finland already offers maintenance plans and periodic inspections, and Finnish providers offer recurring maintenance contracts. #6's opportunity is ongoing coordination and execution; its novelty is lower than previously scored.
- **Economics:** separate gross premiums, service revenue, contribution and avoided claims. The historical illustrations are assumptions, not forecasts or proof of profitability.
- **Tree map:** height and distance may support exposure estimates. They do not establish which tree will fall or justify a felling recommendation by themselves.
- **First Yes and handover:** the first-client benefit is understandable, but operational feasibility remains difficult. Seller incentives for handover are unvalidated rather than assumed absent.

**Chosen pitch:** If Home Watch helps a family share responsibility for an empty home and arrange help at the moment they need it. Demonstrate one complete care-and-visit workflow. Introduce paid visits as a business hypothesis to test; keep broad maintenance subscriptions and new claim guarantees for later evaluation.

## Idea 1 — Water leaks in a newly bought used home

**Problem.** Buyers of used homes can inherit unfamiliar plumbing and appliances, along with protection instructions and age deductions they have not understood. Public water-damage figures show a material prevention opportunity; a higher claim rate specifically in the first year of ownership has not been established here.

**Who / when.** Buyers of homes built roughly 1965–1990; the first 30 days after getting the keys. About 60,000 used homes are sold in Finland a year (KVKL forecast for 2025).

**Evidence**

- About 100 water leaks a day in Finnish homes (If).
- 2021: 34,800 leak claims costing €171M, about the same as all fire claims (€168M) (Finance Finland). No newer figure found.
- The dishwasher is the most common source; If puts the average dishwasher leak at €4,000–5,000, up to €10,000. Pohjola: average detached-house leak €8,500, sometimes over €200,000.
- Leaks peak in detached houses built 1965–69 and apartments from the late 1970s.
- If's home terms (`OMA 200.27`, valid from 1 Jan 2026):
  - §6.2.1.3: pipes 30–50 years old → 25% off the repair payout; over 50 → 50%.
  - §6.2.1.4: annual age deductions of 3% (pipes, cables) and 6% (other plumbing, heating and electrical equipment); "cannot be removed even for an extra fee".
  - §5.5 protection rules: drip tray under washing machine and fridge where there is no floor drain; washing machine shut-off valve closed when not in use and the machine supervised; main valve closed if the home is empty for more than a week.
  - §6.4.2: ignoring protection rules lets If reduce or refuse the claim.
  - §4: water through failed or missing waterproofing, floor-drain joints, wear and slowly developing damage are not covered.
- Pohjola home risk test (44,000+ respondents): ~50% leave the dishwasher running unattended; ~30% wrongly think old-pipe leaks are not covered; only 37% of house owners close the main valve when away.

**What already exists (why it was dropped)**

| Who | What |
|---|---|
| If Norway — Tryggere Hjem | 3 water + 3 smoke sensors (partner Ohma), 24/7 alarm centre, plumber included, no deductible if a sensor triggered. Included in the Super house/cabin policy. |
| If Norway — If Boligsjekk | Anticimex building expert checks moisture, pipes, hoses, roof, drainage, bathroom → condition report. Included in Super. |
| If Sweden × Svensk Fastighetsförmedling | Home buyers get 30 days free move-in insurance, 19% off year one, 250 SEK at a security shop. |
| Länsförsäkringar (SE), Topdanmark, LB Forsikring, Privatsikring (DK), Hiscox (UK) | Free LeakBot leak detector + repair service. Länsförsäkringar pilot: 40% uptake, +50% new home-insurance sales. LeakBot claims 39% fewer claims (vendor figure). |
| Hippo (US) | Free sensors + personalised maintenance plan in an app. |
| Finland | LähiTapiola: free leak detector for some high-risk homes (2021). Pohjola: online home risk test. Uponor/Phyn Plus auto shut-off sold since 2019 (€799). If Finland: guides, a moving checklist and a maintenance plan with periodic inspections for eligible customers. |

| Criterion | Score | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Customer value | 3.5 | Buying a home is a clear life event and unfamiliar systems create a plausible need; perceived urgency needs customer validation. |
| 2. Business value | 3.5 | Prevention and potential acquisition are credible benefits under the brief; incremental savings for this version remain unvalidated. |
| 3. Feasibility | 4.5 | Proven: If Norway, Länsförsäkringar and three Danish insurers already run it. |
| 4. Innovation | 1.5 | It would pitch If Norway's own product back to If. |

**Verdict.** Pitching this would mean pitching If Norway's own product back to If.

## Idea 2 — Home memory handover (seller to buyer)

**Problem.** Every time a home is sold, its memory is lost: the seller knows the dishwasher is 12 years old, the bathroom was redone in 2009 and the main valve is behind the sauna panel; the buyer may receive incomplete instructions and records. If would be the neutral keeper of that handover, with water risk as the first use case.

**What exists.** DIAS digitises the sale contract, not the home's history. Home logbooks exist elsewhere: EU digital building logbook policy, Woningpas (Belgium), BASTA (Sweden), Clea (France). KotiApp (Finnish home-document app) rates 2.3/5.

| Criterion | Score | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Customer value | 3 | Buyers can benefit from practical house knowledge; perceived value and seller participation need testing. |
| 2. Business value | 2.5 | Useful risk data, but the value depends on adoption that may not come. |
| 3. Feasibility | 3 | The software is straightforward, but participation is uncertain. Easier handover and fewer buyer questions are possible seller incentives to test. |
| 4. Innovation | 2.5 | Logbooks exist in the EU, but none from an insurer in Finland; leak prevention exists at If Norway. |

**Verdict.** Parked as a standalone product. Adoption is unvalidated, not proven impossible. Home Watch can use a simpler version: family members collect instructions and photos during their own visits.

## Idea 3 — When a parent dies, If looks after the empty home (merged into #8)

**Problem.** When a parent dies, their home becomes the family's problem overnight, and nobody tells them how to look after it. The state is digitising the paperwork; nobody is looking after the house.

**Evidence**

- 59,209 people died in Finland in 2025, up 942 on 2024 as the population ages (Statistics Finland).
- If receives death information automatically. Its guidance says property insurance can remain in the estate's name until distribution or sale; new owners must arrange insurance after distribution. A death notice does not establish that the home is vacant.
- The estate inventory is due within 3 months; homes and cottages often sit empty for months while heirs, often in other cities, sort things out.
- If's published home terms describe building supervision as a visit at least monthly (§5), and give specific water and heating instructions (§5.5). For more than a week unoccupied, the main valve must be closed and the domestic-water pump switched off; the valve location also matters where there is a meter. In cold weather without sufficient basic heat, systems must be drained and the meter space still heated, with weekly heating checks. Use the policy version applicable to the property. Neglect of protection instructions can affect compensation (§6.4.2).
- UK Financial Ombudsman decisions show estates' water-damage claims refused over unoccupancy clauses; the industry accepts empty homes carry more risk.
- Insurers often demand powers of attorney from every heir, each on its own form (Finnish Parliament written question KKV 528/2021).
- Cottage owners average 61 years old; only 6% are under 40 (MTV; article date not confirmed).

**What already exists**

- **State estate platform (from 1 Feb 2027):** estate inventory, heirs register and authorisations in OmaVero. Paperwork only: nothing about the home, cottage or belongings. Potential register access and timing require verification. Home Watch's first version does not depend on this future infrastructure.
- **UK:** Life Ledger and Settld notify companies of a death; LV= partners with Life Ledger. Notification only.
- **Banks** (OP, Nordea, OmaSP) handle estate banking.
- **If Finland:** FAQ page on estate insurance; nothing proactive found.
- The earlier search did not establish a directly equivalent insurer-led service. Paid home-watch services and related insurer services exist; see #8. This is not evidence of global uniqueness.

**Concept sketch**

- Offer an invitation when the family contacts If about an estate, or through an appropriate verified contact after notification. Confirm vacancy, the coordinator and authority to arrange visits.
- Assign water, heating and visit tasks to a family member, with an option to request an approved local partner. Record observations and completion; the record supports evidence of particular checks.
- Weather watch on the address: FMI frost or storm forecast → alert heirs or book a check.
- One shared view for all heirs.
- Exit: help the family review the plan and arrange appropriate insurance when the property is sold, distributed or kept by an heir. Continuation is a customer choice, not an automatic transfer.

|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Customer value | 4 | A specific life event and an understandable need for families responsible for an empty home. | Demand and ease of use during grief need validation. |
| 2. Business value | 3.5 | Prevention, customer continuity and potential acquisition are credible mechanisms. | Incremental effects and service costs have not been established. |
| 3. Feasibility | 3.5 | Shared tasks, house notes and weather prompts are feasible; visits require a new operating process. | Vacancy confirmation, authority, keys and local partner coverage need validation. |
| 4. Innovation | 3.5 | If helps the family carry out practical home-care responsibilities during a life change. | Home-watch and coordination services already exist; meaningful differentiation must be demonstrated. |

## Idea 4 — "Which tree will fall on your house?" (possible later exposure-map feature)

**Revised concept.** Enter an address to see vegetation whose estimated fall reach could overlap a building. NLS height data and building footprints may support an exposure map; they do not establish which tree will fall, its internal condition or the appropriate intervention. Weather warnings can prompt a professional assessment or a check. This is a later feature, outside the first Home Watch version.

**Fact-check**

| Claim | Result |
|---|---|
| Storm Hannes (Dec 2025) felled up to 4 million m³ of trees | ✅ Finnish Forest Centre's latest estimate (earlier 0.3M, then 3.2–4M m³) |
| ~227,000 households lost power | ✅ Total affected; 180,000+ at once at the peak |
| LähiTapiola paid ~€25M; trees on buildings among main damages | ✅ Yle |
| If: Nordic weather-related insured losses up ~14% in five years | ✅ Tia Mäki, If claims director (Taloustaito, 5 Aug 2026) |
| "Every krone spent on storm preparation saves six" | ✅ Same If claims director, citing a Norwegian estimate |
| NLS laser-scanning data is open | ✅ Open version thinned to 0.5 points/m²: fine for tall trees, too coarse for species or health |

**Stronger evidence not in the original idea.** If's press release of 25 Sep 2026: Hannes caused **ten times the normal monthly number of natural-hazard claims at cottages**; **the main cause was trees falling on buildings**; frozen pipes at cottages often go unnoticed until spring; If advises "check the trees on your plot and fell weak ones near buildings". If received 1,500+ Hannes claims early on; the average Hannes claim was €2,000.

**What already exists.** US insurers use aerial imagery (Nearmap, Cape Analytics) to detect overhanging trees, mostly *against* customers (a Travelers customer in Austin paid $3,000 for tree work to keep her policy). University of Helsinki research built laser-based storm-risk maps for forests, not houses. No consumer service found showing which trees could hit your house.

| Criterion | Score | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Customer value | 3 | Real and fresh after Hannes, but "storms are coming" is a season, not a life event. |
| 2. Business value | 3 | If's own numbers (1:6, ten-fold cottage claims), but average claim only €2,000 and big storms are rare (Hannes was If's second-worst since 2010). |
| 3. Feasibility | 3 | Open height data exists, but individual-tree estimates, data age and exposure accuracy need validation. A pre-processed map is not proof of reliable fall prediction. |
| 4. Innovation | 3 | Flipping underwriting tech to the customer is a nice twist, but alone it reads as a prevention app. |

**Weak points.** Trust ("will If raise my premium if it sees risky trees?"); felling may need a municipal permit in town-plan areas (unchecked); can't see rot; the risky tree may be on the neighbour's land.

## Idea 6 — "Oma taloyhtiö": a housing company for your house (future business expansion)

**Insight.** Finland runs on housing companies. 48% of households (1.36M) live in apartment buildings, where a monthly charge (*vastike*) pays a property manager to keep a long-term maintenance plan (PTS), a maintenance company to do seasonal checks, and the housing company to save for big repairs years ahead. When a family moves to their first detached house, usually when children arrive, all of that disappears overnight: they become the board, the property manager, the caretaker and the repair fund. Insurance does not fill the gap: it covers sudden accidents, not wear or neglect (If terms §4; If Norway's product sheet: "maintenance and improvements" excluded).

**Life event.** Moving from an apartment to your first house. In Finland, 52% of households with more than one person live in detached or semi-detached houses (Statistics Finland 2023); ~2.66M people lived in detached houses (2017).

**Product: one monthly fee ("vastike for your house")**

1. **A maintenance plan for your house:** a one-house PTS built from building data (year, heating, materials), the condition-inspection report from the purchase, and If's claims knowledge of what fails when. Output: a 10-year schedule and cost forecast ("roof ≈ €18k around 2031").
2. **The routine work done on schedule** by local maintenance partners already driving routes to housing companies: gutters, ventilation filters, drains, water heater, frost prep. Weather-triggered tasks from FMI (snow load, storms, frost; reuses the tree map and storm watch from ideas #4–5).
3. **A repair reserve:** an optional monthly saving for the big items, like a housing company's renovation fund (via a partner bank; If is not a bank).
4. **Insurance that fits:** home insurance plus breakdown cover for systems (heat pump, water heater), fixed through If's repair network. Documented maintenance also proves the protection rules were followed, so claims are faster with fewer disputes.

**Business model (all inputs are assumptions)**

Keep these financial measures separate; they are not a combined revenue or profit forecast.

| Measure | Assumption | Illustration at 10,000 houses |
|---|---|---|
| Subscription billings | €39/month | €4.68M/year before tax treatment and service expenses |
| Additional gross insurance premium volume | 50% new to If, €800/year each | €4.0M/year before claims, acquisition and underwriting costs |
| Extra-job contribution | €600/year of separate jobs per house, 15% retained margin | €0.9M/year before additional operating costs |

- Illustrative service arithmetic: €468/year − €150 for two visits − €60 for platform/operations = €258 before remaining costs and tax treatment. Actual visit scope, travel, scheduling, customer acquisition and partner prices need validation.
- The estimate of roughly one million detached and semi-detached households is not a verified addressable market. First-time purchases, service coverage and willingness to pay determine the reachable segment.
- Claims avoided would be a separate benefit. The cited general prevention ratio is not a measured return for this product.
- The HomeServe acquisition is a precedent for the service category, not proof of demand or profitability for this proposed Finnish bundle.
- If could earn service fees and benefit from reduced insured damage. Other service companies can offer similar bundles; no exclusive insurer advantage has been established.

**What already exists**

- **UK/US:** HomeServe repair plans (reactive breakdown cover); Hippo maintenance plans (US).
- **If Finland:** eligible customers already receive an Anticimex maintenance plan, a home visit every four years and telephone advice. The proposed extension is ongoing coordination and completion of work.
- **Finnish maintenance providers:** recurring contracts already exist, including IV-O's scheduled ventilation maintenance and Nurmijärven Rakennushuolto's described detached-house maintenance contracts. The exact proposed bundle has not been established as an existing product.
- **Helen:** historical service-agreement materials describe a Fennia-linked offer; current availability remains unconfirmed.
- **If Norway:** Boligsjekk (one-off inspection), sensors, free building advice. These are building blocks for this idea, not the subscription model.

| Criterion | Score | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Customer value | 4 | Every Finn understands "no more housing company". Willingness to pay not yet validated. |
| 2. Business value | 4 | A direct paid-service mechanism plus potential insurance continuity and prevention. Willingness to pay, costs and incremental acquisition remain assumptions. |
| 3. Feasibility | 3.5 | Existing maintenance providers support feasibility. Coordinating a reliable bundle at the proposed price remains new work; a repair reserve and new breakdown cover add further dependencies. |
| 4. Innovation | 3 | A useful extension into ongoing execution, with clear precedents in maintenance plans, recurring contracts and home-service businesses. |

**Weak points and answers for the judges**

- *"If isn't a service company."* Partners do the field work; If owns the plan, the customer and the risk.
- *"Finns do their own house maintenance."* Target those who don't: young families, dual-income couples, first-time house owners, older owners.
- *"How will you use my house data?"* Define the proposed service's data use and explain it clearly. Pricing or discount commitments require an actual product design.
- *"Rural visits are expensive."* Start where routes are dense (Turku region: Kaarina, Lieto, Raisio).

**48-hour demo**

1. Enter an address in Kaarina → building year and type → a 10-year maintenance plan and the monthly fee.
2. "This month": tasks, partner booking, house health score.
3. FMI storm forecast → "We'll check your roof on Saturday."
4. If dashboard: subscribers, revenue, avoided claims.

## Idea 7 — "If First Yes": help a new entrepreneur win their first paying customers (parked)

**Life event.** Starting a business or becoming self-employed.

**Problem to validate.** A capable new entrepreneur has no customer reviews or delivery history. Customers hesitate to hire them, but they need customers to establish that history: "I can do the work, but nobody knows me yet."

**Product.** An If-backed delivery guarantee that the entrepreneur can include in their own project proposals. It combines verification, clear acceptance criteria, protected payments and a practical recovery route if a covered delivery failure happens. The immediate customer benefit is a stronger reason to say yes to a new entrepreneur.

### Example customer journey

A new freelance developer offers an €800 website to a café. The café likes their work but worries about paying someone inexperienced. The freelancer sends an **If First Yes proposal link**:

1. Both sides agree on a small, clearly defined project: three pages, a working contact form, a delivery date and ownership of the files.
2. The freelancer completes identity and relevant skills verification.
3. The customer funds the project through a payment partner that holds the money.
4. A proposed €49 protection fee activates the delivery guarantee.
5. Payments are released as agreed milestones are accepted.
6. If a covered delivery failure happens, the service arranges qualified help to finish the agreed work, with a defined limit on additional costs.

The customer gets a practical recovery route. The entrepreneur gets a stronger trust signal before they have accumulated reviews. Successful projects build verified delivery history.

### Distinctive proposition and existing alternatives

The proposed differentiator is a **portable first-client guarantee**. The freelancer can use the proposal with customers found through LinkedIn, personal contacts or their own website, across sales channels. A launch version could focus on the first few eligible contracts.

Escrow is already established: Upwork offers protected direct contracts. The novelty to defend is the combination of verified ability, a portable first-client guarantee and help completing the work when delivery fails. This needs competitor and customer validation; no world-first claim has been established.

If already markets insurance to new entrepreneurs, giving the concept a plausible connection to its existing business. The delivery guarantee would require a newly designed product; existing If insurance should not be assumed to cover it.

### Business model

**Primary revenue:** a protection fee on each eligible contract, paid by the freelancer, customer or shared between them. The initial product should earn money through protected contracts themselves. Later business-insurance purchases are an additional opportunity, not the basis of the initial revenue model.

**Illustrative unit economics — every figure below is an assumption, not researched pricing or a forecast:**

| Item | Assumed amount per contract |
|---|---:|
| Protection fee collected | €49 |
| Expected cost of covered failures | −€15 |
| Verification, payment and support costs | −€14 |
| **Contribution before acquisition, capital and fixed costs** | **€20** |

The €800 project payment belongs to the service transaction; it is not If's revenue. Money refunded from unreleased customer funds must be distinguished from recovery costs paid under the guarantee.

The commercial test is whether protection increases successful hiring enough to justify the fee while keeping recovery and support costs manageable.

**Potential distribution:** entrepreneurship programmes, invoicing platforms and vocational schools. These are proposed channels, not confirmed partners. Distribution through existing channels avoids needing to launch a new freelance marketplace.

### Main risks and launch scope

- **Fair acceptance criteria:** begin with small projects and objective deliverables, such as a specified website with a working contact form and transferred source files.
- **Delivery failure and recovery:** define covered failures, remedies and limits on additional completion costs. A qualified recovery network is essential.
- **Verification and disputes:** establish relevant skills verification and independent review; a customer disagreement is not automatically a covered failure.
- **Fraud and adverse selection:** test whether the product attracts unusually risky projects or collusive claims, and limit exposure during a pilot.
- **Customer acquisition:** test the cost of reaching first-time entrepreneurs against the small number of initial protected contracts.

### Hackathon prototype and validation

Demonstrate one complete protected contract: create the proposal, agree on deliverables, show verification and the protection fee, accept milestones, then simulate a delivery failure and recovery. Payment, underwriting, guarantee issuance and recovery should be presented as prototype flows rather than live capabilities.

Test the same proposal with potential customers, with and without the guarantee. Measure changes in willingness to hire and willingness to pay. Interview new entrepreneurs about whether the protected proposal would help them secure their first clients.

**Pitch:** "Your first customer is hard to win when nobody knows your work. If First Yes gives new entrepreneurs the backing to earn that first opportunity."

**Verdict.** A proposed commercial direction with a direct transaction fee and a clear life-event connection. Demand, differentiation, recovery costs and underwriting feasibility remain unvalidated.

## Idea 8 — If Home Watch (selected)

**Pitch:** "When a parent's home is left empty, If Home Watch helps the family share responsibility, take the right precautions and arrange a visit when nobody can go."

**Initial customer:** adult children or other authorised family members responsible for a parent's home after a death. Begin with a confirmed vacant home in one local service area. A surviving household member may still live there, so vacancy must be confirmed.

**Human problem:** the family is grieving, busy and often far away. They may not know the house's systems or the care it needs. A reminder can go unanswered because nobody has accepted responsibility.

**Customer outcome:** the family can see what needs attention, who has agreed to handle it, when the next visit is due and how to arrange help. The home's useful knowledge and visit records stay together.

### Evidence and differentiation

- If's published home terms define building supervision as at least a monthly visit (§5) and include specific water and heating precautions (§5.5). The plan must use the terms applicable to the home. Neglecting protection instructions can affect compensation (§6.4.2). [If home-insurance terms](https://www.if.fi/globalassets/fi/pdf/ehdot/kotivakuutusehdot.pdf)
- If receives death information automatically, but confirmation, family contact and authorisation are separate steps. Its guidance describes authority to manage estate insurance and says new owners must arrange insurance after distribution. [If estate guidance](https://www.if.fi/henkiloasiakkaat/asiakaspalvelu/usein-kysyttya/kuolinpesan-vakuutukset)
- Existing home-watch providers already inspect vacant homes and produce reports with photos. Coordination apps and cottage books also exist. These are precedents, not evidence that care and documentation are new inventions.
- If Finland already offers eligible homeowners maintenance plans and periodic inspections. If Norway's Tryggere Hjem combines sensors, an alarm centre and conditional deductible relief, with automatic claim creation. [If Finland maintenance service](https://www.if.fi/henkiloasiakkaat/vakuutukset/kotivakuutus/omakotitalo/omakotitalon-kunnossapitosuunnitelma), [If Norway Tryggere Hjem](https://www.if.no/privat/forsikring/bolig/tryggere-hjem)

**Differentiation to demonstrate:** connect a specific life event to an understandable care plan, shared responsibility, practical local help and useful records in one experience. Global uniqueness has not been established. The prototype should show why this connection helps the family.

### First-version workflow

1. **Invite and confirm.** Offer Home Watch when the family contacts If about an estate, or through an appropriate verified contact. Confirm the address, whether anyone still lives there, the family's coordinator and who can authorise property access.
2. **Create the care plan.** Ask for the known water and heating setup and show the next important actions with their reasons. Unknown details stay marked as unconfirmed. Use the applicable policy instructions and source references.
3. **Assign responsibility.** Every urgent task has a named owner, a due time and a visible status. Family members can accept a visit or say they cannot attend. An unaccepted task remains visible for the coordinator to resolve.
4. **Build the house notes.** During the first visit, record where the water valve, meter, fuse box and relevant pump are. Add photos, short instructions and useful contacts.
5. **Turn warnings into action.** A cold-weather or storm forecast prompts a relevant check. The forecast does not establish the indoor temperature or prove damage. If nobody can visit, the coordinator can request a local partner.
6. **Approve a visit.** Show the proposed service, price, availability and access arrangements. The authorised person approves the booking. Supplier availability and any attendance commitment need actual partner agreements.
7. **Record and share.** After a family or partner visit, store who attended, when, what they checked, photos, observations and outstanding actions. Each completed task updates the shared plan.
8. **Review the transition.** When the home is occupied, sold or distributed, close or adapt the care plan and help the new owner arrange appropriate insurance. Continuation requires their choice.

**Prototype data:** one fictional property, sample family roles, sourced task templates and a sample forecast. Label simulated bookings and notifications in the demonstration. Access to confidential If systems, actual estate registers or real partner booking APIs is not required for the prototype.

### Visit records and claim-related benefit

The visit log is a record of specific observations and actions. It can help the family explain what was checked if a claim happens.

**A possible future commitment to discuss with If:** agree in advance which evidence it will accept for particular protection steps. This requires an evidence standard and an approved product design. A photo alone does not establish continuous heating, every precaution or causation.

The first-version promise is practical care and clear records. Claim decisions still depend on the insured event, applicable cover, exclusions, limits, deductibles, age deductions and other conditions. Present the log as the family's useful record; avoid a compliance badge implying the house is fully safe or every loss covered.

### Business model to validate

| Mechanism | Proposed value for If | Evidence needed |
|---|---|---|
| Optional paid visits | Earn a transparent coordination fee or an agreed share of the visit price | Family willingness to pay, provider quotes, availability and actual service costs |
| Prevention | Reduce avoidable damage or detect it earlier | Baseline empty-home claims and a measured comparison over time |
| Customer continuity | Help an heir arrange suitable cover when ownership changes | Incremental continuation compared with the existing estate process |
| Acquisition | Introduce invited family members to useful If service | Voluntary conversion and acquisition costs |
| Service efficiency | Make property observations and completed checks easier to find | Time saved for families and, where applicable, customer-service or claims staff |

**Commercial starting hypothesis:** offer the shared plan as part of the proposed service and charge for optional approved visits. Test one clearly defined visit package in a compact local area before considering a monitoring subscription. No prices, commission rates or partner commitments are validated.

**Financial discipline:** service-fee revenue = completed paid visits × the fee If earns per visit. Provider payments are separate from If's retained fee, depending on the commercial arrangement. Subtract coordination, support, acquisition, service-liability and other relevant costs to assess contribution. Keep service revenue, insurance premiums and avoided claims separate; none is automatically profit. The estate model in #3 does not establish a Home Watch revenue or savings forecast.

### First version and later opportunities

**Build first:** the vacancy and coordinator setup, ordered tasks, shared visit plan, house notes, a weather-triggered task, an optional approved visit request and the resulting visit record.

**Later opportunities:** a parent's move into care, a newly inherited cottage, recurring visits and a validated tree-exposure map. The occupied-home maintenance subscription from #6 is a separate expansion hypothesis. Repair savings accounts, new breakdown cover and new claim-evidence commitments need their own validation.

### Prototype demonstration

1. A family member opens an estate-support invitation and confirms that the fictional home is empty.
2. They see a short care plan, invite a sibling and assign the first visit.
3. The sibling adds a water-valve photo and records the completed check.
4. A sample forecast shows a cold night approaching. The heating check is overdue and nobody has accepted it.
5. The coordinator requests and approves a priced local visit with agreed access.
6. A sample visit report arrives: heating observed, photos added and any concern left as an open task.
7. The family sees the updated plan and next visit. A record can be retrieved or exported if needed.

**Closing slide:** "The family knows who is looking after the house — and can get help when nobody can go."

### Validation before a real pilot

- Ask people who have managed a relative's empty home which responsibilities were missed, how visits were arranged and whether they would use this workflow.
- Test whether users can identify the next urgent task, find the valve instructions, assign a visit and recognise an unanswered task.
- Obtain quotes and operating requirements from local providers: scope, keys, travel, availability, reporting and responsibility for the work.
- Validate the task templates with If against the relevant policies; confirm the contact and authority process.
- Measure setup completion, accepted tasks, overdue visits, paid-visit conversion and contribution per completed visit. Longer-term claims and retention effects need separate measurement.

**Decision:** proceed with If Home Watch and the focused family-home-after-loss scenario. The other ideas remain background research and possible expansion paths.
